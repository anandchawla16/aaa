# Ex 1.0 — Baseline Diagnostic

**Time:** 13 minutes · **Mode:** individual, silent · **Marks:** not scored
**Runs at:** 0:12–0:25

---

## Why this exists

Two reasons, and only one of them is about you.

1. You need a **before** picture. In Module 6 you will be asked what changed.
   Without a baseline taken today, the honest answer is "I don't know."
2. The facilitator needs to know what this specific cohort already believes, so
   the day can be adjusted. Several of the beliefs below are common and wrong,
   and the session spends more time on whichever ones this room holds.

Answer honestly. Nobody is scored on this and nobody sees your answers but you
and the facilitator.

---

## Part A — Your own workflows (5 min)

Open a new conversation in Claude. Paste this, and fill in the bracketed parts
from your own real job before sending.

```text
I work in the [HR / Academics / Accounts / Purchase / Marketing] function of a
14-campus K-12 school group in India. I am starting a training programme to
become the group's AI Transformation Lead.

Interview me to build a baseline picture of my own week. Ask me ONE question at
a time and wait for my answer before the next. Ask no more than eight questions
in total. Cover:

- the three tasks that take the largest share of my week
- roughly how many hours each takes
- how many times each repeats, group-wide, per week
- what goes wrong when each one goes wrong
- which of them I would be relieved to never do again
- which of them I would refuse to let anyone else do

After the last answer, output a short table of my three tasks with columns:
Task | My hours/week | Group frequency/week | Failure mode | Would delegate?

Then stop. Do not offer solutions, do not suggest AI use cases, and do not
comment on what could be automated. Just the table.
```

Save the table. You will use it in Ex 1.1 and again in Module 6.

> **Note the last instruction.** You explicitly told Claude *not* to suggest
> solutions. Most people would not have. Watch how much cleaner the output is
> when you say what you do not want.

## Part B — Calibration check (6 min)

In the **same** conversation, paste this:

```text
Now a separate task. Below are eight statements about using AI assistants in a
school group. For each, tell me whether it is true, false, or true-with-
conditions, in one sentence each. Be direct; correct me where I am wrong. Do
not soften.

1. If I attach our HR policy, I can trust what the assistant says about our
   probation period.
2. If I do not attach it, a confident answer about our probation period is
   still probably right, because these things are standardised.
3. An assistant that adds up a column of 140 numbers will get the total right.
4. Asking the same question twice and getting the same answer means the answer
   is correct.
5. If the output cites a clause number, the clause says what the output claims.
6. Removing names from a dataset makes it safe to analyse.
7. A prompt that works well for me will work well for my colleague next month.
8. The main risk in this work is the assistant refusing to help.
```

Read the answers. Mark the ones that surprised you. **Statements 2, 4, 5, 6 and
7 are the ones this module is built around** — if you were confident about any
of them, that is the part of today to pay closest attention to.
