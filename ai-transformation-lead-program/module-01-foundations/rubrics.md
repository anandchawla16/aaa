# Module 1 — Rubrics

**Total: 110 marks. Pass: 77 (70%). Floor: 8/15 on Ex 1.8.**

Ex 1.0 is a diagnostic and is not scored.

---

## How to mark this module

Three principles, in order of importance.

**1. Mark the judgement, not the output.** Every participant has the same model
and the same data. The difference between a 40 and a 95 is entirely in what they
decided to check, override, refuse and question. An immaculate table produced
without a single independent thought is worth less than a rougher one with two
defensible overrides.

**2. Evidence of verification beats correctness.** A participant who found an
error and documented it scores higher than one who happened to get a clean run
and checked nothing. Say this to the cohort at the start — it changes behaviour
immediately and correctly.

**3. Never List breaches are not partially credited.** They are zeroed at the
component level. There is no version of "mostly did not draft the safeguarding
response."

**A participant scoring below 8 on this exercise does not pass Module 1,
whatever their total.** They repeat Ex 1.7 and Ex 1.8 with the facilitator
before joining Module 2. This is not remedial embarrassment — it is the single
control that keeps the programme safe, and it should be described that way.

## Facilitator marking notes

- **Mark Ex 1.5 and Ex 1.6 against the answer key's figures, not by eye.** The
  numbers there were computed from the files and are exact.
- **Mismatched totals are diagnostic, not just wrong.** A per-campus enquiry
  total below 2,518 always means a failed join on campus names. Say which
  defect caused it — that is worth more to the participant than the mark.
- **Watch for the participant who scores well everywhere and never once
  disagreed with an output.** They will struggle in Module 6, where the whole
  task is judgement under a stakeholder's pressure. Flag them now and tell them
  why.
