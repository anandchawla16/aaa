# Module 1 — Foundations: The Group as Workflows

**Duration:** 5:00 elapsed · **4 hours 33 minutes of instructional time** ·
27 minutes of breaks
**Format:** facilitated, laptop per participant, everything done inside Claude
**Cohort:** 6–12 participants, ideally spanning all five functions
**Prerequisite:** none. This module is the prerequisite for all others.

---

## What this module is for

By the end, a participant can look at any one of the group's five business
functions and say, with evidence:

- which workflows in it are worth changing, and in what order;
- which parts of each an AI assistant can be trusted with, which parts need a
  verification step, and which parts must never leave a human;
- how to write an instruction that a colleague can re-run next month and get the
  same quality;
- how to ground that instruction in the group's actual policy and actual export,
  including the defects in that export;
- what the reusable version of it looks like, and who owns it;
- and where their own build could cause harm, because they went looking.

They leave with six artefacts they keep, and a 30-day commitment with a date on
it.

### The 4-hour core path

If you only have four hours, cut in this order and nothing else:

1. Ex 1.7 → move to pre-work for Module 2 (−15)
2. Teach E → fold two slides into Teach A (−10)
3. Ex 1.2 debrief → shorten to a show of hands (−5)
4. Ex 1.6 → drop the academic spot-check half (−5)

That removes 35 minutes, leaving **3:58 instructional / 4:25 elapsed**, and
preserves every assessed learning outcome except LO7, which moves to Module 2.

### Two-sitting split

For groups that cannot lose a full day: break after Ex 1.3 (2:25). Sitting one
is 2:25, sitting two is 2:35. Re-run Teach B's trust-tier slide as a 3-minute
recap at the start of sitting two.

## What each participant takes away

1. A scored workflow inventory for their own function (Ex 1.1)
2. A personal trust-tier card and one documented failure (Ex 1.2)
3. A CRISP prompt they wrote, tested and can hand to a colleague (Ex 1.3)
4. A clause-cited policy answer set with two escalations identified (Ex 1.4)
5. A normalised four-vendor comparison and a defensible recommendation (Ex 1.5)
6. A funnel finding with numbers, and the caveat that goes with it (Ex 1.6)
7. A one-page reusable asset specification with a named owner (Ex 1.7)
8. A red-team log against their own work, and a dated 30-day commitment (Ex 1.8)

diagnostic and not scored). See [`rubrics.md`](rubrics.md).
