# Role Charter — AI Transformation Lead, School Group

## Why this role exists

A school group does not have an AI problem. It has a **throughput problem in
five functions** — HR, Academics, Accounts, Purchase, and Marketing &
Admissions — where a small number of people at Group Office serve fourteen
campuses through processes that are manual, undocumented, and inconsistent
between sites.

Most of the work in those functions is language work: reading, drafting,
comparing, summarising, reconciling, chasing. That is exactly the class of work
a capable AI assistant can carry — under supervision, on grounded data, inside
rules that a human sets.

The AI Transformation Lead is the person who makes that happen **without**
breaking the things a school cannot afford to break: a child's safety, a
parent's trust, an employee's rights, an auditor's trail.

---

## What the role is

> The AI Transformation Lead finds high-friction, high-volume workflows in the
> group's five business functions, redesigns them so that an AI assistant does
> the drafting and analysis while a named human keeps the judgement and the
> signature, proves the value on real numbers, and makes the result something an
> ordinary campus administrator can run every week without help.

## What the role is not

| Not this | Because |
|---|---|
| A software engineering role | If a solution needs a developer to maintain it, it will not survive at a campus. The default build surface is Claude itself. |
| A tools-evaluation role | Nobody is being asked which vendor to buy. The question is which workflows to change. |
| An innovation-showcase role | A demo that impresses the Board and is never used again is a failure, and will be scored as one. |
| A decision-making role | The Lead never becomes the person who decides an admission, a salary, a vendor award or a disciplinary outcome. |
| An IT support role | Access, licensing and device issues route to IT. |

---

## Accountabilities

1. **Workflow portfolio.** Maintain a live inventory of candidate workflows
   across the five functions, scored on volume, friction, risk and readiness.
2. **Redesign and build.** Convert selected workflows into reusable assets that
   run inside Claude — grounded prompts, Projects, Skills, and artifacts — and
   hand them to the function owner.
3. **Evidence.** Measure before and after against a Board priority. Hours
   returned, days of overdue balance removed, cost per admission, three-quote
   compliance. Not "adoption".
4. **Guardrails.** Own the group's AI use rules: what may be pasted where, what
   must be grounded, what is never delegated, and what the audit trail looks
   like.
5. **Capability.** Train the function teams. The Lead's success measure is how
   much runs without the Lead.
6. **Escalation.** Bring to the Board the cases where AI use would cross a line
   — and be the person who says no.

## Boundaries — the Never List

These are not delegated to an AI assistant, drafted by one for direct send, or
decided with one, at any maturity level:

- Any student safeguarding, child protection or disciplinary matter.
- Any decision to admit, reject, or withdraw a student.
- Any decision to hire, not confirm, promote, or terminate an employee.
- Salary fixation, appraisal ratings, and disciplinary findings.
- Award of a contract or purchase order.
- Any communication that carries a legal threat or a statutory representation.
- Anything involving a named child's health, behaviour, or family circumstances.

The Lead may use AI to **prepare, structure, summarise or draft** inputs into
these decisions where policy allows. The decision, the wording sent out, and
the signature remain human. Module 1 makes this the first thing you learn, not
the last.

## Who the Lead works with

| Stakeholder | What they need from the Lead | What the Lead needs from them |
|---|---|---|
| **Group CFO** | Overdue balance down; audit trail intact | Fee policy, ledger access, waiver authority |
| **Group HR Head** | Faster letters and responses; retention data | Policy versions, approval routes |
| **Group Academic Director** | Teacher admin load down, no classroom disruption | Access to coordinators, calendar protection |
| **Group Purchase Head** | Three-quote compliance up, comparisons faster | Requisitions, quotes, committee calendar |
| **Group Marketing Head** | Cost per admission explained and reduced | CRM export, spend data, counsellor time |
| **Principals** | Nothing new to maintain; no surprises to parents | Honest reporting of what is not working |
| **IT & Compliance** | No data leaving approved systems | Approved Systems List, consent position |
| **Board of Trustees** | Value on the six priorities; no reputational risk | A quarterly, evidence-based read-out |

---

## How the Lead is measured

At six months, against the Board's own priorities — not against activity.

| Measure | Target at 6 months |
|---|---|
| Workflows redesigned and in weekly production use | ≥ 6, across ≥ 4 of the 5 functions |
| Verified hours returned per week, group-wide | ≥ 120 |
| Movement on at least two Board priorities | Demonstrated with before/after numbers |
| Assets running without the Lead's involvement | ≥ 70% of those deployed |
| Guardrail breaches | Zero tolerated; every near-miss documented |
| Function owners who can run and modify their own asset | ≥ 1 per function |

**A note on the last row.** A Lead who becomes indispensable has failed. The
role is to make five functions independently capable, then move to the next
five workflows.
