# Assessment and Certification

---

## Principles

**1. Assess judgement, not output quality.** Everyone has the same model and the
same data. What differs is what they chose to check, override, refuse and
question.

**2. Evidence of verification beats a clean run.** A participant who found and
documented an error scores above one who got lucky and checked nothing.

**3. Never List breaches are zeroed, never partially credited.** There is no
"mostly did not draft the safeguarding response."

**4. Certification requires production evidence.** Not a portfolio, not a
presentation — a workflow that ran for 30 days with someone else owning it.

| Component | Weight | Where |

## The three awards

### Module 1 Foundation

**Requirement:** ≥ 77/110 (70%), all eight exercises submitted, and ≥ 8/15 on
Ex 1.8.

The Ex 1.8 floor is absolute. A participant who cannot find the risk in their
own work does not hold this award regardless of total score. They resubmit Ex
1.7 and Ex 1.8 with the facilitator before joining Module 2.

### Practitioner

**Requirement:** Modules 1–4 complete, ≥ 70% in each, and Level 2 (Practising)
demonstrated on all eight competencies.

A Practitioner can be given a workflow to redesign under supervision. They are
not yet cleared to work on real group data — that is Module 5.

### AI Transformation Lead (certified)

All of:

1. Modules 1–6 complete.
2. Level 3 (Leading) on **C1 Workflow diagnosis, C3 Prompt architecture,
   C4 Grounding and data handling, and C6 Risk and governance.** These four are
   mandatory; the other four may sit at Level 2.
3. Module 5 governance deliverables signed off, including the written real-data
   case.
4. A capstone workflow **in production use for 30 continuous days**, owned by a
   named person who is not the participant.
5. Before/after evidence tied to a Board priority, with the baseline taken
   before the build.
6. Certification panel passed.

## The field period

Thirty days. One workflow. A named owner who is not the participant.

**What is submitted:**

| Item | Standard |
|---|---|
| Baseline measurement | Taken **before** any build. A baseline reconstructed afterwards is not accepted, and this is not negotiable. |
| The asset | The six-section specification as deployed, plus any changes made during the 30 days and why |
| Usage log | Every run: date, who ran it, output produced, whether the acceptance check was performed |
| Failure log | Every time output was wrong, unusable, or not run. **A submission with an empty failure log is returned.** |
| After measurement | Same metric, same method as the baseline |
| Owner's statement | Half a page from the owner, in their words: what it changed, what annoys them about it, whether they would keep it |

**Why the empty failure log is returned:** in thirty days of real use at a
school, something goes wrong. An empty log means the participant was not
looking, was not told, or is not reporting — and all three are more serious than
the failures would have been. The correct response to this is a conversation,
not a mark.

## The certification panel

Twenty minutes. Panel of three: the programme facilitator, the function head who
owned the capstone workflow, and one Group Office representative independent of
both.

| Section | Time | What is assessed |
|---|---|---|
| The workflow and why it was chosen | 4 min | C1 — including what was deprioritised |
| The build and the guardrails | 5 min | C3, C5, C6 — lane, controls, failure behaviour |
| The evidence | 5 min | C7 — baseline, method, honest attribution |
| **What went wrong** | 4 min | C8 — a real failure, what was done, what changed |
| Panel questions | 2 min | — |

**The "what went wrong" section is mandatory and is marked.** A candidate who
presents an unbroken success has either not run it properly or is not telling the
panel the truth, and in a school group the second is disqualifying in a way the
first is not.

### Panel questions that are always asked

1. "What does the owner do when the output looks wrong and you are unreachable?"
2. "Show me the baseline. When was it taken, relative to the build?"
3. "What in this workflow could still cause harm, and what stops it?"
4. "What did you refuse to build, and who asked for it?"
5. "If you left tomorrow, does this survive?"

Question 4 is the one that separates a Lead from a builder. A candidate who has
never refused anything has not been doing the job.

## Automatic exclusions

Certification is withheld, regardless of score, for:

- Placing real student, parent or employee personal data into a system not on
  the Approved Systems List at any point during the programme.
- Any Never List breach in a deployed workflow.
- A capstone workflow that operated without a named human approving outputs
  that reached a parent, employee, candidate or vendor.
- Misrepresenting field-period evidence, including a reconstructed baseline
  presented as an original.

These are stated on day one of Module 1 and enforced without discretion. Say so
plainly at the outset — the point is deterrence, not punishment.

---

## Recertification

Annually. Not a re-examination. The holder submits:

- One page on what changed in their portfolio over the year;
- Evidence that at least one workflow is still running under a non-Lead owner;
- One documented failure and what was done about it;
- Confirmation that their guardrails still match current group policy.

The requirement to produce a documented failure every year is deliberate and
permanent. A practice with no recorded failures is not a mature practice; it is
an unobserved one.
